Read this before you place anything

A column is a node. All five holes in a column on the same side of the centre channel are the same electrical point. Two component legs in the same column are connected; the same column on the other side of the channel is a different node.

The ESP32 is not drawn pin by pin, on purpose. 30-pin and 38-pin DevKits order their headers differently, and vendors differ even within 38 pins. Find each pin by its silkscreen label, then use any free hole in that pin's column on that side of the board — it is the same node as the pin. Put the module over the low columns (roughly 1–20) so columns 21+ stay free for parts.

Board size. This layout assumes a full-size 63-column breadboard. On a half-size 30-column board, put the ESP32 on one board and the parts on a second, and carry the two rails across with a pair of jumpers.

HC-SR04 and its divider

  • The sensor's four pins go in four consecutive holes: VCC, TRIG, ECHO, GND.
  • ECHO does not reach a GPIO directly. It goes through 1k to a junction column, and from that column through 2k to ground.
  • The ESP32 reads the junction column.

ECHO swings to 5 V and ESP32 GPIOs are NOT 5 V tolerant. Most tutorials wire it straight to a pin; that is how people quietly cook an ESP32. The divider brings it to about 3.3 V.

The finished board

  • Everything in place. Compare yours hole by hole before you plug in USB.
If the E-stop latches on its own, step 5 is missing or on the wrong column. If nothing lights at all, check the sensor — without it the sketch idles forever waiting for a reading.
