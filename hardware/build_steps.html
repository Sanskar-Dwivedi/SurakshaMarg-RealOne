ESP32 build guide

Bench governor — build it step by step

Eight steps. Read every diagram left to right: each box is one thing you push into the breadboard, and each line between two boxes is one jumper wire. Chains start at an ESP32 pin and end at a rail. Where a line drops to the row below, that is a branch off the same node — not a new connection in series.

Want the same circuit placed on an actual board, hole by hole? See the breadboard build.

Two steps exist only to protect the board

Step 5 (10k pull-up on the E-stop) and step 7 (1k/2k divider on ECHO). Most tutorials skip both. Skipping the first gives you an E-stop that fires at random; skipping the second puts 5 V into a pin that is not 5 V tolerant.

Pins are named by their silkscreen label. GPIO36 and GPIO39 are printed VP and VN on most boards — the diagrams show both.

1

Power the rails

  1. 3V3 to the + rail.
  2. GND to the − rail.
  3. Link the top and bottom − rails together.
Find every pin by the label printed on the board, never by counting holes. The 30-pin and 38-pin DevKits order their headers differently.
2

Three status LEDs

  1. 220Ω from GPIO26, then the green LED, then the − rail.
  2. Same again for GPIO27 (red) and GPIO14 (amber).
  3. Long leg is the anode and faces the resistor. Short leg goes to ground.
Green = permitted, red = refused, amber = gave up and called a human. The blue ARMED heartbeat is GPIO2, already soldered to the board.
3

The emitter

  1. 220Ω from GPIO25.
  2. Piezo after it: + to the resistor, − to the rail.
If your transducer is a 40 kHz ranging element it is the wrong part — 40 kHz is above the 35 kHz cattle audiogram endpoint. Fine as a signal-chain stand-in; just say so.
4

The three veto buttons

  1. GPIO32 → PERSON button → − rail.
  2. GPIO33 → DOG/GOAT, GPIO35 → E-STOP, same pattern.
  3. No resistors needed on the first two — the sketch uses INPUT_PULLUP.
Press-to-ground is the right way round for a safety input: a broken wire reads as ‘not pressed’ rather than as a stuck button.
5

Pull up the E-stop

  1. 10kΩ from the GPIO35 node to the + rail.
  2. Same node as the button leg — the resistor sits alongside it, not in series.
GPIO34–39 are input-only and have no internal pull-up in the silicon. Without this the input floats and the E-stop latches at random, usually mid-demo. It looks like flaky software, not a missing resistor.
6

Group-size potentiometer

  1. Outer legs to + and −.
  2. Wiper to GPIO34.
  3. Sets how many animals the governor thinks are grouped, 1 to 8.
The pot goes to 3V3, never 5V. ESP32 ADC pins are not 5 V tolerant.
7

HC-SR04, through a divider

  1. VCC to VIN/5V — the sensor needs 5 V to be reliable.
  2. GND to the − rail.
  3. TRIG straight to GPIO13. That is an output, so 3.3 V is fine.
  4. ECHO → 1kΩ → junction → 2kΩ → ground. GPIO39 (VN) reads the junction.
ECHO swings to 5 V and ESP32 GPIOs are NOT 5 V tolerant. Most tutorials wire it straight to a pin; that is how people quietly cook an ESP32.
8

Power up and check

  1. Plug in USB. The on-board blue LED blinks about once a second.
  2. Serial monitor at 115200: the limits print, then NO calibrated mic attached: this rig makes no dB claim.
  3. Turn the pot fully left first, or it refuses for herd size straight away.
  4. Hand about 80 cm from the sensor → green, PERMITTED @ 28.6 m.
If the E-stop latches on its own, step 5 is missing or on the wrong node. If nothing lights at all, check the sensor — without it the sketch idles forever.
